Chapter 3 'Zhurong's Fire Prison' (祝融火狱): the underground forge-palace and prison of Zhurong (祝融), the fire god of Chinese myth, deep under a volcano: black basalt pillars, black-iron halls, giant bronze ding cauldrons and furnaces, hanging chains, fire altars and rivers of lava. The god's fire has run wild and turned his smiths, jailers, forge-made constructs and fire beasts into fire fiends. Palette: black iron and basalt black, molten lava orange and vermilion lacquer red, with dark bronze; the corrupting fire is a deep molten lava-orange glow with yellow-hot cores, glowing from cracks, seams, mouths and vents (never the white-gold sun fire of chapter 1).

Character: 神火飞鸦, about 0.6 m tall. A divine fire crow (神火飞鸦), the Ming-dynasty winged rocket bomb shaped like a crow, brought to life in the fire god's forge as a flying kamikaze: a crow-shaped construct about 1.6 m in wingspan made of a light bamboo frame covered in black lacquered paper, with real crow proportions (stout body, thick strong beak, fanned tail), big painted fierce red-and-gold eyes, vermilion paper wing edges; its fat round belly is a paper-and-bamboo gunpowder drum glowing lava orange through the paper seams (weak spot: the whole belly), and under each wing hang two small bamboo rocket tubes with paper fuses that it flies with. Little bamboo-and-wire legs with four clawed toes. Twitchy, shrieking, explosive.
