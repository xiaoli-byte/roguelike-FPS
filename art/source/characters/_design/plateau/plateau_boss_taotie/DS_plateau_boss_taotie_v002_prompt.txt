Chapter 4 'Snowland Sky Road' (雪域天路): the high grassland plateau of western Sichuan and eastern Tibet, a wide green summer meadow basin ringed by snow peaks and a glacier, with a turquoise holy lake, a glacial river, stone watchtowers and colourful prayer flags. In myth the Yellow Emperor flayed Kui (夔), the one-legged thunder beast, to make a war drum; Kui's thunder soul slept in the holy lake and has now woken: thunderstorms sweep the plateau, and its marmots, yaks and the strange beasts of the Shan Hai Jing's Western Mountains are charged with corrupted thunder. Creature palette: plateau animal colours (greyish brown, cream white, rusty red, soot black) with weathered copper and bone accents; the corrupting thunder is a magenta-violet lightning glow with white-hot cores, showing as glowing zig-zag veins, crackling fur tips, glowing eyes and the weak spot (never the white-gold sun fire of chapter 1, the cyan-violet frost of chapter 2 or the lava orange of chapter 3). No people, no religious figures or symbols.

Character: 饕餮, about 5 m tall. Taotie (饕餮), the insatiable glutton of Chinese myth whose face is the 兽面纹 / taotie motif cast on Shang and Western Zhou ritual bronzes (like the Houmuwu ding and the bronze gong vessels), and the Shan Hai Jing glutton with 'eyes under its armpits, tiger teeth and human-like claws'. A squat, massive, low beast about 5 m tall at the shoulder, built like a living Shang bronze vessel: NO fur at all; the whole body is cast bronze with thick verdigris-green and dark brown patina, worn golden highlights on the raised edges, every surface filled with fine low-relief squared cloud-and-thunder spirals (云雷纹). Its HEAD is the classic taotie mask, perfectly symmetric and as wide as its chest: a raised vertical flange ridge (扉棱) down the middle of the face, two huge bulging round eyes with pupils set in long almond eye sockets with hooked corners (臣-shaped), big C-shaped horns curling outward like the motif's horns, flat curling brows and ears, and a wide lower jaw hanging open with tiger fangs, sculpted lake water pouring into it as it swallows a lake. Its body is short and barrel-like, the belly a deep bronze cauldron (鼎) shape with ridged flanges along the spine; four thick column legs like the legs of a square ding, ending in clawed human-like hands. Its real eyes: two glowing magenta-violet eyes set in the armpits, under the front legs, clearly visible in the main pose where it rears up with one foreleg raised (weak spots). Thin magenta lightning glows in the cracks of the patina. Distinctly ancient CHINESE Shang dynasty bronze, NOT an Aztec, Mayan or Mesoamerican stone mask, not a furry bear or yak, not a realistic person.

Stylization: cartoon game proportions in the same family as the earlier rosters (jerboa soldier, fire rat, sancai tomb beast, huodou sighthound): chunky simplified masses, oversized heads and paws, toy-like readable forms with clean bevelled edges. Lightning is painted as OPAQUE glowing magenta-violet zig-zag veins and solid sculpted spark shapes on the body (never transparent bolts, particles, smoke or glow halos); fur, hair and feathers as a few large carved clumps.
