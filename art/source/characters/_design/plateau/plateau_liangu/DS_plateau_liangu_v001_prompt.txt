Distinctly Chinese mythological fantasy. Memorable at 30 metres; one obvious glowing weak spot.

Chapter 4 'Snowland Sky Road' (雪域天路): the high grassland plateau of western Sichuan and eastern Tibet, a wide green summer meadow basin ringed by snow peaks and a glacier, with a turquoise holy lake, a glacial river, stone watchtowers and colourful prayer flags. In myth the Yellow Emperor flayed Kui (夔), the one-legged thunder beast, to make a war drum; Kui's thunder soul slept in the holy lake and has now woken: thunderstorms sweep the plateau, and its marmots, yaks and the strange beasts of the Shan Hai Jing's Western Mountains are charged with corrupted thunder. Creature palette: plateau animal colours (greyish brown, cream white, rusty red, soot black) with weathered copper and bone accents; the corrupting thunder is a magenta-violet lightning glow with white-hot cores, showing as glowing zig-zag veins, crackling fur tips, glowing eyes and the weak spot (never the white-gold sun fire of chapter 1, the cyan-violet frost of chapter 2 or the lava orange of chapter 3). No people, no religious figures or symbols.

Character: 连鼓灵, about 2.4 m tall. The Liangu (连鼓灵), a living fragment of the legendary thunder drum made from the hide of Kui: a hovering ring about 2.4 m across of SIX small barrel drums with weathered copper studs and grey-blue hide heads painted with spiral cloud-and-thunder patterns, evenly spaced and joined by a ring of twisted bronze; one of the six drums is lacquered red. At the centre a glowing magenta-violet thunder core (weak spot) is held in a cage of pale beast bone, and one long drumstick carved from a beast bone hangs from the ring. It floats with nothing below it. An animated object: no face, no limbs, no person.

Stylization: cartoon game proportions in the same family as the earlier rosters (jerboa soldier, fire rat, sancai tomb beast, huodou sighthound): chunky simplified masses, oversized heads and paws, toy-like readable forms with clean bevelled edges.
