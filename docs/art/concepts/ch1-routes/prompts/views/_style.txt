Repaint this 3D whitebox render into a finished in-game screenshot concept for a stylized first-person shooter set in the Dunhuang / Silk Road desert of the Tang dynasty. Rendering style: stylized painterly 3D game art (clean readable shapes, hand-painted textures, soft gradients, gentle bevels) — NOT photoreal, NOT noisy, NOT blurry, no purple-grey cast.

STRICT LAYOUT RULES — this is a composition and lighting study of an existing, already-approved level layout:
- Keep the camera, horizon line, lens and every mass of the reference EXACTLY where it is: the same buildings, walls, columns, gates, dunes, cliffs, mounds, openings and paths at the same screen positions, sizes and heights.
- Do not add, remove or move any structure in the playable area; do not invent new doorways, stairs, bridges or roads; do not close any gap the player can walk through.
- Replace the flat whitebox colours with final materials, and add only: surface detail, small scatter at the base of things (pebbles, dry grass tufts, sand drifts against walls, broken bricks), distant background beyond the playable area (far dunes, mesas, haze, sky, clouds), lighting, fog and ambient effects.
- Keep the sun direction implied by the shadows in the reference.

Colour legend of the reference render:
grey-brown flat ground = hard gravel desert floor (black Gobi gravel); light tan = loose sand and dunes; pale tan strip = packed caravan road; brown boxes and walls = rammed-earth (hang-tu) walls and ruins with horizontal tamping layers; reddish-brown masses = sandstone rock, yardangs and cliffs with horizontal strata; dark grey slabs = grey clay-tile roofs with upturned eaves; dark red-brown cylinders, beams and frames = weathered timber columns, beams and gates; light grey boxes = carved stone (steles, platforms, shrines, statues); olive-green cylinders and columns = trees (white poplars, tamarisk shrubs); teal = spring water; muddy beige = dry riverbed.

Art direction (the studio's level-art rules):
- One clear focal point per view (the landmark, or the encounter area ahead): it is the brightest or highest-contrast area; near ground and framing elements are darker.
- Layered atmospheric perspective: distant layers step back into warm haze, plus a low dust band hugging the ground; the sky is the brightest part of the picture.
- Backlit silhouettes when the sun is ahead of the camera; cast shadows divide the ground and frame the focal point.
- Restrained, low-saturation desert palette with a single accent colour.
- A low-contrast environmental motion layer: blowing sand streaks, drifting dust motes, a banner or two, a few distant birds.
- No text, no labels, no UI or HUD, no weapon or hands, no people unless stated.
